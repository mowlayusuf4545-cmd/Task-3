# ADR Causality Assessment: A Pharmacovigilance Project

## Project Summary

Causality assessment is an important part of pharmacovigilance because
it helps evaluate whether an adverse event may be associated with a
medicine. This project explores how the timing of a reaction, the
patient's clinical course, withdrawal of the suspected medicine, and
other possible explanations can be considered when reviewing a suspected
adverse drug reaction (ADR).

The learning activity focuses on interpreting case information and
describing the relationship between a suspected medicine and an adverse
event using recognised causality assessment approaches.

## Project Aims

-   Explain the purpose of causality assessment in pharmacovigilance.
-   Review the time interval between medicine exposure and the onset of
    a reaction.
-   Consider whether the reaction improves after the suspected medicine
    is stopped.
-   Check for other medicines, diseases, or factors that could explain
    the event.
-   Understand how causality categories are assigned using structured
    assessment methods.
-   Recognise the value of clear ADR documentation and reporting.

## Assessment Factors

The following information can help when evaluating a suspected ADR:

1.  **Time relationship:** Determine whether the event began after the
    medicine was started.
2.  **Dechallenge:** Review whether the event improved after the
    suspected medicine was withdrawn.
3.  **Rechallenge:** If the medicine was restarted for a valid clinical
    reason, consider whether the event returned. Rechallenge should not
    be performed solely to prove causality when it could put the patient
    at risk.
4.  **Other explanations:** Consider the patient's illness, other
    medicines, and non-drug causes.
5.  **Clinical findings:** Review symptoms, examination findings,
    laboratory results, and the patient's medical history.
6.  **Known safety information:** Check whether the reaction has
    previously been associated with the medicine.

## Common Causality Categories

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Category                            General interpretation
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  **Certain**                         The evidence strongly supports a
                                      causal link, and other explanations
                                      have been reasonably excluded.

  **Probable / Likely**               The timing and clinical course
                                      support a drug-related reaction,
                                      while alternative explanations are
                                      unlikely.

  **Possible**                        The timing is compatible with a
                                      drug-related reaction, but another
                                      cause may also explain the event.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The exact criteria vary by method. Commonly used approaches include the
**WHO--UMC causality assessment system** and the **Naranjo Adverse Drug
Reaction Probability Scale**.

## Example Case for Learning

**Patient:** 45-year-old woman\
**Suspected medicine:** Amoxicillin\
**Reported event:** Skin rash\
**Reported onset:** Three days after treatment began\
**Reported outcome:** Recovery after the medicine was discontinued

### Initial Review

-   **Timing:** The rash appeared after amoxicillin treatment was
    started.
-   **Withdrawal:** Improvement after stopping the medicine may support
    an association.
-   **Other possible causes:** An infection, another medicine, or an
    unrelated skin condition should also be considered.
-   **Preliminary interpretation:** The information is compatible with a
    possible association, but it is not enough to confirm causality. A
    complete history and formal assessment are needed before assigning a
    final category.

*This example is fictional and intended for learning. It should not be
treated as a confirmed clinical case or an actual ADR report.*

## Why Causality Assessment Matters

A structured review of suspected ADRs can help healthcare professionals
to:

-   Recognise and document potential medicine-related harm.
-   Support appropriate ADR reporting.
-   Improve medication safety and clinical decision-making.
-   Identify patterns that may require further drug-safety
    investigation.
-   Contribute to ongoing monitoring of the benefit--risk profile of
    medicines.

## Learning Reflection

Through this activity, I developed a clearer understanding of how the
chronology of a reaction, response to medicine withdrawal, competing
explanations, and clinical evidence contribute to ADR causality
assessment. The exercise also highlighted the importance of careful
documentation and responsible pharmacovigilance practice.
